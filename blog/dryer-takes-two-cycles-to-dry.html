Why Does My Dryer Take Two Cycles to Dry? 6 Causes, Ranked

Before you call an appliance repair company, run through this list. Four of the six causes are things you can check in ten minutes, and the most likely one is a clogged vent.

1. Restricted vent (most likely)

Lint in the duct or at the exterior cap. Test: run the dryer and check the outdoor flapper. Weak airflow or a flap that barely moves confirms it. Fix: full vent cleaning.

2. Crushed or kinked transition hose

The flexible hose behind the dryer gets flattened when the dryer is pushed back. Test: pull the dryer out and look. Fix: replace with semi-rigid aluminum and leave room behind the dryer.

3. Dirty lint screen or softener film

Dryer sheets leave an invisible film that blocks airflow even when the screen looks clean. Test: run water over the screen; if it pools instead of flowing through, it's coated. Fix: scrub with dish soap and a brush.

4. Overloaded or over-wet loads

A washer that isn't spinning well leaves clothes too wet. Test: clothes should be damp, not dripping, out of the washer. Fix: smaller loads, higher spin speed.

5. Failed heating element or gas igniter

The dryer runs but air isn't hot. Test: run on high heat for five minutes and feel the exhaust air at the cap or inside the drum. Cool air means a heating problem. Fix: appliance repair.

6. Moisture sensor coated with residue

Sensor-dry cycles stop early or run forever when the metal sensor bars inside the drum are coated with softener. Fix: wipe the bars with rubbing alcohol.

The order to do things

Check the hose and the screen, then test the vent airflow. If airflow is weak, get the vent cleaned before paying for an appliance call. Most repair techs will tell you the same thing after charging a diagnostic fee.

Related questions

How do I know if my dryer vent is clogged?

The five most common signs are clothes taking more than one cycle to dry, the dryer or laundry room feeling very hot, a burning smell while the dryer runs, lint building up around the outdoor vent cap or behind the dryer, and the outdoor vent flap not opening while the dryer runs.

Why does my dryer take two cycles to dry clothes?

A restricted vent is the number one cause. When airflow drops, moist air can't leave the drum, so clothes stay damp and the dryer runs longer. If the lint screen is clean and the dryer still takes two cycles, the vent is almost always the culprit.

Why is the top of my dryer hot to the touch?

Heat that should be exhausting outdoors is backing up because the vent is restricted. A dryer with good airflow stays warm, not hot. This is a safety warning sign, not just an annoyance.

Why does my laundry room smell like burning when the dryer runs?

Lint sitting on or near the heating element is scorching. Stop using the dryer and book a cleaning. A burning smell is one of the last warnings before a dryer fire.

Why is there lint on the outside of my house near the vent?

Lint escaping around the cap means the cap, flapper, or screen is partially blocked and exhaust is finding another way out. The inside of the duct is usually worse than what you see.

My dryer vent flap doesn't open when the dryer runs. What does that mean?

Airflow is too weak to push the flapper open, or the flapper is stuck shut with lint. Either way exhaust is being forced back into the duct. This is a reliable visual test you can do yourself in two minutes.
