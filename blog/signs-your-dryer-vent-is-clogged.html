9 Signs Your Dryer Vent Is Clogged (and What to Do About It)

Your dryer rarely fails all at once. It slows down. If you've started running a second cycle without thinking about it, the vent is probably the reason. Here are the signs we hear most often from Twin Cities homeowners, in rough order of how urgent they are.

1. Clothes take more than one cycle to dry

This is the number one complaint. When lint restricts the duct, moist air can't leave the drum, so clothes stay damp and the dryer runs longer. If your lint screen is clean and a normal load still needs 90 minutes, the vent is restricted.

2. The dryer is hot to the touch

A dryer with good airflow feels warm on top, not hot. Heat that should be going outside is backing up into the cabinet. This is a safety sign, not just an efficiency one.

3. A burning smell while the dryer runs

Lint is sitting on or near the heating element and scorching. Stop using the dryer and book a cleaning. A burning smell is one of the last warnings you get before a fire.

4. Lint around the outdoor vent cap

Walk outside and look at the cap. Lint hanging from the louvers or matted on a screen means exhaust is struggling to exit. The inside of the duct is almost always worse than what you can see.

5. The outdoor flapper doesn't open

Run the dryer and watch the cap. The flap should swing open and you should feel a strong, warm stream of air. If it barely moves, airflow is weak or the flap is glued shut with lint.

6. The laundry room is humid

Moist exhaust is leaking back into the room, usually from a crushed or disconnected transition hose behind the dryer. Look for condensation on windows or lint on the floor behind the dryer.

7. The dryer shuts off mid-cycle

Modern dryers have thermal fuses that trip when the cabinet overheats. Many customers call us right after an appliance repair tech replaced the fuse and said "get your vent cleaned."

8. Your energy bill crept up

A restricted dryer can use 30% more energy per load. Over a year of family laundry that is roughly $80–$120 in wasted electricity or gas.

9. It's been more than a year

Even without symptoms, the lint is there. Manufacturers and the U.S. Fire Administration recommend annual cleaning. Roof vents and heavy-use households should be on a six-month schedule.

The two-minute test

  1. Clean the lint screen and start the dryer on high heat with a damp towel inside.
  2. Go outside to the vent cap. The flapper should be fully open with a strong stream of warm air you can feel from two feet away.
  3. If the flap is barely open, the air is weak, or you see lint at the cap, you have a restriction.

Related questions

How do I know if my dryer vent is clogged?

The five most common signs are clothes taking more than one cycle to dry, the dryer or laundry room feeling very hot, a burning smell while the dryer runs, lint building up around the outdoor vent cap or behind the dryer, and the outdoor vent flap not opening while the dryer runs.

Why does my dryer take two cycles to dry clothes?

A restricted vent is the number one cause. When airflow drops, moist air can't leave the drum, so clothes stay damp and the dryer runs longer. If the lint screen is clean and the dryer still takes two cycles, the vent is almost always the culprit.

Why is the top of my dryer hot to the touch?

Heat that should be exhausting outdoors is backing up because the vent is restricted. A dryer with good airflow stays warm, not hot. This is a safety warning sign, not just an annoyance.

Why does my laundry room smell like burning when the dryer runs?

Lint sitting on or near the heating element is scorching. Stop using the dryer and book a cleaning. A burning smell is one of the last warnings before a dryer fire.

Why is there lint on the outside of my house near the vent?

Lint escaping around the cap means the cap, flapper, or screen is partially blocked and exhaust is finding another way out. The inside of the duct is usually worse than what you see.

My dryer vent flap doesn't open when the dryer runs. What does that mean?

Airflow is too weak to push the flapper open, or the flapper is stuck shut with lint. Either way exhaust is being forced back into the duct. This is a reliable visual test you can do yourself in two minutes.
