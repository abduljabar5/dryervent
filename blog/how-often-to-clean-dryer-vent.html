How Often Should You Clean Your Dryer Vent? A Minnesota Schedule

The short answer is once a year. The honest answer depends on how your house is built and how you do laundry. Here is how we help customers set a schedule.

Start with the baseline: every 12 months

The U.S. Fire Administration, the National Fire Protection Association and nearly every dryer manufacturer recommend having the exhaust duct cleaned annually. For a household doing 4–6 loads a week with a straight, short run to a wall vent, that's the right interval.

Move to every 6 months if any of these apply

  • Roof vent. Lint falls back down the vertical run and mats against the cap. Roof vents clog about twice as fast as wall vents.
  • 8+ loads a week. Big families, cloth diapers, sports uniforms. More loads, more lint.
  • Pets. Fur and dander produce dramatically more lint than fabric alone.
  • Duct over 25 feet or 3+ elbows. Every elbow is a collection point.
  • Gas dryer. Same lint, but a blockage also backs up carbon monoxide. Err on the side of caution.
  • Older flex duct. Ridged foil or vinyl duct traps lint; until it's replaced, clean more often.

Why Minnesota is harder on vents

Cold caps condense the moisture in dryer exhaust, so lint arrives damp and sticky and packs harder. Snow drifts bury low wall vents and freeze flappers. Spring brings birds looking for warm 4-inch holes. A fall cleaning before heavy winter use is the best single appointment of the year; roof vents benefit from a second visit in spring.

Between cleanings

  • Clean the lint screen every load and wash it monthly with dish soap to remove softener film.
  • Check the outdoor flapper opens while the dryer runs.
  • Keep shrubs and snow 12 inches from the cap.
  • Make sure the transition hose isn't crushed behind the dryer.

Related questions

What is dryer vent cleaning?

Dryer vent cleaning is the removal of lint and debris from the entire duct that runs from the back of your dryer to the vent cap outside your home. We use a rotating brush system and a high-powered vacuum to scrub the full length of the duct, clear the exterior cap, and verify airflow afterward. It is different from cleaning the lint screen, which only catches a portion of the lint your dryer produces.

How often should a dryer vent be cleaned?

Once a year for most households, and every six months if you do more than 8 loads a week, have pets, own a roof-vented dryer, or have a duct longer than 25 feet. The U.S. Fire Administration and most dryer manufacturers recommend annual cleaning as the baseline.

Why does lint build up in the vent if I clean the lint trap every load?

The lint screen only catches about 75% of lint. The rest travels down the duct with the hot, moist exhaust air, sticks to the duct walls, and packs tightest at elbows, screens, and the exterior cap. Over a year a typical family's duct collects a few cups to several gallons of lint.

How long does dryer vent cleaning take?

Most wall or ground-level vents take 45–60 minutes. Roof vents take 60–90 minutes because we clear the cap from the roof and clean the vertical run from both ends. Bird nest removal or a very long duct can add 20–30 minutes.

Does dryer vent cleaning really make a difference?

Yes. The most common result customers notice is that dry times drop from two cycles back to one. A clean vent restores airflow, lowers the dryer's operating temperature, cuts energy use by up to 30%, extends the life of the heating element and motor, and removes the number one cause of dryer fires.

What is the difference between dryer vent cleaning and air duct cleaning?

Dryer vent cleaning cleans the single 4-inch exhaust duct from your dryer to the outdoors. Air duct cleaning cleans the HVAC supply and return ductwork for your furnace and AC. They require different tools and we specialize only in dryer vents, so you get a technician who does this all day.
