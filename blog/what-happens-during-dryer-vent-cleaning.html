What Happens During a Professional Dryer Vent Cleaning (Step by Step)

If you've never had it done, here is the whole appointment, start to finish. Wall vents take about 45–60 minutes; roof vents 60–90.

1. Arrival and walk-through (5 min)

We confirm where the vent exits, look at the laundry setup, and lay a drop cloth behind the dryer. For roof vents we pick a ladder spot.

2. Baseline airflow reading (2 min)

We run the dryer and measure exhaust velocity at the cap with an anemometer. Restricted vents often read under 500 feet per minute; a clean 4-inch vent reads roughly 1,200–1,800. This goes on your receipt.

3. Disconnect and inspect the transition hose (5 min)

We slide the dryer out, disconnect the hose and check it for crushing, lint and material. Foil or vinyl gets flagged; we offer semi-rigid aluminum as a $39 replacement.

4. Rotary brush and vacuum (20–40 min)

A HEPA vacuum attaches to the duct at the dryer end. Flexible rods with a nylon brush are fed the full length of the duct, spinning to scrub the walls while the vacuum captures everything. We work in stages, pulling back to clear the brush, and reverse direction for stubborn plugs.

5. Exterior cap (10 min)

At the wall or on the roof, we remove matted lint, pull any screen, remove nests, and make sure the damper swings freely. We photograph the cap before and after.

6. Reconnect and seal (5 min)

Hose reconnected with foil tape and a clamp, dryer slid back with the hose uncrushed.

7. Final airflow test and show-and-tell (5 min)

We run the dryer again and take the after reading. Then we show you the lint, usually one to three gallons, and point out anything you should know about: flex duct, a long run, a cap that should be replaced.

8. Payment and receipt

Pay by card, cash, Zelle or Venmo. Your emailed receipt includes both airflow numbers and the cap photos. Book your appointment.

Related questions

What happens during a dryer vent cleaning appointment?

We confirm the vent exit, pull the dryer out and disconnect the transition hose, attach a high-powered vacuum, and run a rotating brush through the full duct length. We clear the exterior cap, reconnect the hose, test airflow before and after, and show you the lint we removed.

How do you measure airflow?

We use an anemometer at the exterior cap or a manometer at the dryer. A healthy 4-inch dryer vent moves exhaust at roughly 1,200–1,800 feet per minute; a restricted one may be under 500. We note the before and after numbers on your receipt.

Will you move my dryer?

Yes, we slide it out to reach the connection and slide it back when we're done. If the dryer is stacked or in a very tight closet we may clean from the exterior instead.

Do you need access to the inside of my home?

Yes for a complete job. Cleaning only from outside can push lint back toward the dryer. We need about 3 feet of space in front of the dryer.

What tools do you use for dryer vent cleaning?

A rotary brush system with flexible rods that reach 35+ feet, a HEPA-filtered vacuum, a reverse-airflow line for stubborn plugs, a camera for inspection, and an airflow meter. We don't use leaf blowers or compressed air alone, which just redistribute lint.

Do you take before and after photos?

We photograph the exterior cap before and after on every job and include them in your emailed receipt. A full camera inspection with interior duct photos is an optional $29 add-on.
