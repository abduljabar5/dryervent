Roof vs. Wall Dryer Vents: How to Tell Which One You Have

Every dryer vent ends somewhere outside your house. Where it ends determines how fast it clogs, how it gets cleaned and what you pay. Here's how to find yours.

Step 1: Where is your laundry room?

Basement or main floor on an exterior wall: the duct almost always goes straight out the wall nearby, 1–4 feet above the ground. Second floor or an interior room: the duct usually runs up through the attic and exits the roof.

Step 2: Walk the outside of the house

On the laundry room side, look for a 4-inch hooded cap with a hinged flap. For basement laundry it's near the foundation. For upstairs laundry on an exterior wall it may be high on the siding. Run the dryer and feel for warm air.

Step 3: Check the roof

No cap on any wall? Look at the roof for a small gray or black hood, roughly the size of a coffee can, often on the rear slope. That's a roof dryer vent. Don't confuse it with the larger plumbing stacks, which are open pipes without a hood.

Related questions

How do I know if my dryer vents through the roof?

Look outside for a vent cap near your dryer's side of the house. If there is no 4-inch cap on any exterior wall and your laundry is on an upper floor or an interior wall, look at the roof for a small hooded cap, usually gray or black. During booking we show animated pictures of both so you can pick.

Why do roof dryer vents clog faster?

Hot moist air has to travel straight up, and lint falls back down when the dryer stops, so lint stacks at the bottom elbow and mats against the cap screen at the top. Roof caps also have a damper or screen that catches lint and is rarely inspected because no one goes on the roof.

Do you go on the roof to clean the vent?

Yes, when roof pitch and weather allow. We clear the cap, remove any screen or nest, and run the brush down from the top while vacuuming at the dryer. On very steep or icy roofs we clean from inside with an extended rod system and inspect the cap with a camera.

Can you clean a roof dryer vent in winter?

Yes. We clean from inside the home with an extended brush system and verify flow at the cap. If the roof is safe to walk we clear the cap from above; if it is iced, we schedule a quick free follow-up visit to check the cap when it melts.

Is it safe to have a dryer vent on the roof?

It is allowed by code and common in two-story homes, but it is the highest-maintenance vent type. We recommend cleaning roof vents every 6–12 months and replacing screened caps with a louvered dryer-specific cap.

What is the best dryer vent cap for a roof?

A dryer-specific roof cap with a large hood, a free-swinging damper, and no screen. Screens trap lint. We can install a pest-resistant cap for $89 that keeps birds out without blocking lint.
